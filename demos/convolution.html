Convolution: one neuron, slid across an image

Chapter 3. The nine numbers below are the weights of one neuron looking at a 3×3 patch. Sliding it over every position gives a feature map. You know this as filtering with a kernel. In a convolutional network the only difference is that nobody types the numbers in: gradient descent finds them.

Things to try

  • Vertical edge is exactly the neuron from chapter 1: −1 on the left, +1 on the right. Mid-grey means zero response. Bright means dark-to-bright going rightwards; dark means the reverse.
  • Tick ReLU. Now the neuron only fires for one polarity of edge. To catch both, a network needs two filters. That is why real conv layers learn dozens.
  • Type in your own weights. Try making a detector for diagonal edges, or for isolated bright dots.
  • The first layer of AlexNet learned filters much like these (oriented edges, colour blobs) with no one telling it to. Deeper layers apply filters to stacks of feature maps, finding patterns of patterns.
