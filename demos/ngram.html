Shannon's counting language model

Chapter 5. The simplest possible language model: look at the last n characters, look up everything that ever followed them in the training text, and pick one in proportion. The training text here is this book itself. It is "predict the next token" with a lookup table where a neural network should be.

What might come next?

Things to try

  • Step the memory up from 0 to 10, generating at each. At 0 you get letter-frequency soup. At 2 to 3, pronounceable non-words. At 5 to 6, real words in ungrammatical strings. At 8 to 10, fluent phrases.
  • But watch the "seen only once" figure climb. At long memory, most contexts have occurred exactly once, so the model has no choice: it is reciting the book, not generalising. That is the curse of dimensionality from chapter 5. A counting model cannot know that two different contexts are similar.
  • Use One character and watch the bar chart: this distribution over the next token is exactly what a neural language model outputs. The difference is that a network computes it from learned representations, and so can give sensible probabilities for contexts it has never seen.
