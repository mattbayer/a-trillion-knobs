Gradient descent

Chapter 2. The map is the space of weights (here just two). Darker is lower loss. At each step: measure the slope, step downhill by learning rate × gradient. Click anywhere to choose a starting point.

Things to try

  • Stretched bowl: raise the learning rate slowly. Around 0.15 the path starts zig-zagging across the steep direction; just past 0.167 it diverges. The steepest direction sets the speed limit, while the shallow direction is where you actually need to travel. This is why conditioning matters.
  • Now set the learning rate to about 0.03 and add momentum of 0.9. The heavy ball damps the zig-zag and accelerates along the valley floor.
  • Bumpy: with no noise, start in several places: you get trapped in whichever dimple is nearest. Add noise and momentum: the path can rattle out of shallow dimples and find deeper ones. In two dimensions traps are common; in a billion dimensions they are rare (chapter 2 explains why).
  • Curved ravine: plain gradient descent crawls along the curved valley floor. Momentum helps a lot. Adaptive optimisers such as Adam were invented for landscapes like this.
